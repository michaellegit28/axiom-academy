# Statistics

Statistics is the study of collecting, presenting, summarising, and interpreting data.

## Data Collection
Learn the difference between a population and a sample, and why sampling methods affect the conclusions drawn from data.

## Tables & Charts
Study frequency tables, bar charts, histograms, pie charts, line graphs, and appropriate ways to display different kinds of data.

## Mean, Median & Mode
The mean is the total divided by the number of observations. The median is the middle value after ordering the data. The mode is the most frequent value.

**Worked example:** For `2, 3, 3, 5, 7`, mean = `20/5 = 4`, median = `3`, mode = `3`.

## Range & Variation
The range is maximum minus minimum. Use measures of spread alongside a measure of centre when comparing data sets.

## Data Interpretation
Read scales carefully, identify trends and unusual values, and distinguish what the data shows from conclusions that require additional evidence.

### Quick review
Choose a suitable summary or chart for the data type, calculate carefully, and interpret the result in context.
