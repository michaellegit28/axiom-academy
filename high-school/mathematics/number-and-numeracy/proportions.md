## Learning objectives
Recognize direct and inverse proportion, calculate constants of proportionality, and apply proportional reasoning to real situations.

## What is proportionality?
Two quantities are proportional when their relationship follows a fixed mathematical rule. Direct proportion has the form
$$y=kx,$$
where $k$ is constant. The ratio $y/x$ is therefore constant.

## Direct proportion
If 5 notebooks cost ₦10, then one costs ₦2 and 12 cost ₦24. Scaling one quantity by a factor scales the other by the same factor.

If $y=18$ when $x=6$ and $y\propto x$, then $k=18/6=3$, so $y=3x$. At $x=10$, $y=30$.

## Recipe and scale problems
A recipe for 4 people uses 300 g of rice. For 10 people:
$$300\times\frac{10}{4}=750\text{ g}.$$

## Inverse proportion
Two quantities are inversely proportional when
$$y=\frac{k}{x},$$
so $xy=k$ is constant. If 6 workers take 10 days, the idealized time for 12 workers is $60/12=5$ days.

## Distinguishing proportional relationships
A graph of direct proportion passes through the origin. A relationship such as $y=2x+3$ is not directly proportional because $y/x$ is not constant.

## Common mistakes
- Assuming any two changing quantities are proportional.
- Using direct proportion when the product should remain constant.
- Scaling in the wrong direction.
- Ignoring units and practical assumptions.

## Practice
1. If 7 pens cost ₦14, what do 15 cost?
2. $y\propto x$, with $y=18$ when $x=6$. Find $y$ when $x=10$.
3. A recipe for 6 people needs 450 g of flour. How much for 14?
4. $y\propto x$ and $y=20$ at $x=4$. Find $k$.
5. Six machines complete a job in 8 hours. How long would 12 take under ideal inverse proportion?
6. If $xy=72$ and $x=9$, find $y$.
7. Is $y=5x$ direct proportion?
8. Is $y=5x+1$ direct proportion? Explain.
