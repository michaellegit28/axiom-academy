# Proportions

## Learning objectives
- Recognize proportional relationships.
- Use equivalent ratios and scaling factors.
- Solve direct and inverse proportion problems.

## Direct proportion
Two quantities are directly proportional when one is a constant multiple of the other: $y=kx$.

Example: If 5 notebooks cost $10$, one notebook costs $2. At the same rate, 12 cost $24$.

## Scaling
If a recipe for 4 people needs 300 g of rice, a recipe for 10 people needs $300\times10/4=750$ g.

## Inverse proportion
When one quantity increases as another decreases so their product stays constant, $y=k/x$.

If 6 workers take 10 days for a fixed task, the idealized time for 12 workers is $60/12=5$ days.

## Common mistakes
- Assuming every relationship is proportional.
- Using a scaling factor in the wrong direction.
- Forgetting that inverse proportion requires a fixed product.

## Practice
1. If 7 pens cost $14, what do 15 pens cost at the same rate?
2. $y$ is directly proportional to $x$ and $y=18$ when $x=6$. Find $y$ when $x=10$.
3. Six identical machines complete a task in 8 hours. How long would 12 take under ideal inverse proportion?

## Answers
1. $30$.
2. $30$.
3. $4$ hours.
