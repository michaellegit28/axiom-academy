## Learning objectives
Distinguish approximation from estimation, round values appropriately, estimate calculations, and use estimates to check exact answers.

## Approximation
Approximation replaces a value with a nearby value that is easier to use. The symbol $\approx$ means “approximately equal to”. For example, $398\approx400$.

The required accuracy determines the rounding method: nearest whole number, decimal place, significant figure, or another specified unit.

## Estimation
Estimation uses convenient approximations to obtain a sensible answer quickly. It is useful for mental calculation and for checking calculators or algebraic work.

For $49.7\times20.2$, use $50\times20=1000$. An exact result close to 1000 is plausible; a result near 100 or 10,000 should be investigated.

## Estimating with fractions
Use simple nearby values. For example, $398/19.8\approx400/20=20$.

## Bounds and reasonableness
An estimate is not normally the exact answer. Its purpose is to indicate scale and detect major errors. The best estimate is often the simplest value that retains enough information for the purpose.

## Common mistakes
- Giving an estimate as if it were exact.
- Rounding too aggressively before a calculation where accuracy matters.
- Choosing approximations that make the calculation harder instead of easier.
