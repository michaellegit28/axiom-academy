# Ratios

## Learning objectives
- Interpret ratios as comparisons of quantities.
- Simplify ratios and divide quantities in a given ratio.
- Solve ratio problems in real contexts.

## Core idea
A ratio compares quantities measured in the same units. The ratio $a:b$ means that for every $a$ parts of the first quantity there are $b$ parts of the second.

## Simplifying ratios
Divide every part by the greatest common factor. For example, $18:24=3:4$.

## Worked example
A class has 15 boys and 20 girls. The ratio of boys to girls is $15:20=3:4$.

If a recipe uses flour and sugar in the ratio $5:2$ and contains 350 g of flour, one part is $350/5=70$ g, so sugar is $2(70)=140$ g.

## Dividing a quantity
To divide $£84$ in the ratio $3:4$, there are 7 total parts. One part is $84/7=£12$. The shares are $£36$ and $£48$.

## Common mistakes
- Comparing quantities in different units without converting first.
- Adding ratio terms instead of using the total number of parts.
- Treating a ratio as a fraction without checking what each quantity represents.

## Practice
1. Simplify $28:42$.
2. Divide 96 in the ratio $5:3$.
3. A map scale uses a ratio of 1:50,000. What real distance does 4 cm represent?

## Answers
1. $2:3$.
2. 60 and 36.
3. 200,000 cm = 2 km.

## Key review
A ratio is a comparison. Simplify by dividing all parts by the same factor; for sharing, find the total number of parts first.
