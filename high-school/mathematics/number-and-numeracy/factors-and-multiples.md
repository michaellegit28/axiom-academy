## Learning objectives
Define factors and multiples, test divisibility, list factors efficiently, and use prime factorisation.

## Factors
A factor divides a number exactly. The positive factors of 12 are $1,2,3,4,6,12$. Factors occur in pairs: $1\times12$, $2\times6$, $3\times4$.

## Multiples
A multiple is obtained by multiplying a number by an integer. Multiples of 9 include $9,18,27,36,45,\ldots$.

## Divisibility tests
Useful tests include: divisible by 2 if even; by 5 if ending in 0 or 5; by 10 if ending in 0; by 3 or 9 if the digit sum is divisible by 3 or 9; by 4 if the last two digits form a multiple of 4.

## Prime factorisation
Write a composite number as a product of primes. For example,
$$60=2^2\times3\times5.$$
Prime factorisation is the foundation for HCF and LCM.

## Worked examples
**1.** Factors of 36 are $1,2,3,4,6,9,12,18,36$.

**2.** $84=2\times42=2^2\times21=2^2\times3\times7$.

## Common mistakes
- Calling 1 a prime number; it has only one positive factor.
- Confusing factors with multiples.
- Omitting factor pairs.

## Practice
1. List the positive factors of 24.
2. List the first five positive multiples of 7.
3. Is 7 a factor of 91?
4. Write 72 as a product of primes.
5. Is 1 a factor of every positive integer?
6. Is 462 divisible by 3?
7. Is 735 divisible by 5 and 10?
8. Find the number whose prime factorisation is $2^3\times3\times5$.
