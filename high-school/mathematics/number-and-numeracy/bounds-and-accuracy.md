## Learning objectives
Find lower and upper bounds from stated rounding, distinguish bounds from exact values, and use bounds to reason about calculated quantities.

## What is a bound?
A rounded or measured value represents a range of possible exact values. The lower and upper bounds describe that range.

If a length is recorded as 7.4 cm to the nearest 0.1 cm, then
$$7.35\le L<7.45\text{ cm}.$$
The lower bound is included; the upper bound is excluded under the usual rounding convention.

## Finding bounds
The rounding interval is half the rounding unit on either side of the stated value. For a value rounded to the nearest whole number, the interval width is 1 on each side by 0.5:
$$50\text{ to nearest whole number}\Rightarrow49.5\le x<50.5.$$

For 12.6 kg to the nearest 0.1 kg:
$$12.55\le x<12.65.$$

## Limits of accuracy
The stated measurement precision determines the interval. A value measured to the nearest centimetre is less precise than one measured to the nearest millimetre.

## Bounds in calculations
For positive quantities, minimum and maximum possible results can be found using appropriate combinations of lower and upper bounds. For example, if $x$ and $y$ are positive, the minimum possible product uses lower bounds and the maximum uses upper bounds.

## Common mistakes
- Using the full rounding unit instead of half the unit.
- Treating a stated rounded value as exact.
- Forgetting which bound is open under ordinary rounding.

## Practice
1. Give the bounds for 12.6 kg rounded to the nearest 0.1 kg.
2. A value is 50 to the nearest whole number. Give its bounds.
3. A length is 8.2 cm to the nearest 0.1 cm. Give its bounds.
4. A mass is 4.50 kg to the nearest 0.01 kg. Give its bounds.
5. Why are bounds useful in measurement?
6. If $x=20$ to the nearest whole number, can the exact value be 19.49? Explain.
7. Find the maximum of $x+y$ if $x=5.2$ and $y=3.7$, each to the nearest 0.1.
8. Explain why the upper bound is normally excluded.
