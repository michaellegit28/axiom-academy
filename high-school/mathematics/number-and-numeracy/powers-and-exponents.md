## Learning objectives
Interpret powers, use positive, zero, negative and fractional indices, and simplify expressions using the laws of indices.

## Meaning of a power
$a^n$ means multiply $a$ by itself $n$ times. In $a^n$, $a$ is the base and $n$ is the exponent. For example, $2^4=16$.

## Laws of indices
For suitable non-zero bases:
$$a^m a^n=a^{m+n},\qquad \frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n},\qquad (a^m)^n=a^{mn}.$$
Also,
$$a^0=1,\qquad a^{-n}=\frac1{a^n}.$$

For products and quotients:
$$(ab)^n=a^nb^n,\qquad\left(\frac ab\right)^n=\frac{a^n}{b^n}.$$

## Fractional indices
For positive $a$,
$$a^{1/n}=\sqrt[n]{a},\qquad a^{m/n}=\sqrt[n]{a^m}.$$
Thus $16^{1/2}=4$ and $27^{2/3}=9$.

## Scientific connection
Powers of ten are used in standard form and scale calculations. Exponents provide a compact way to represent very large and very small quantities.

## Common mistakes
- Adding exponents when bases are different.
- Treating $(a+b)^2$ as $a^2+b^2$.
- Forgetting that a negative exponent means a reciprocal.
- Confusing $(-2)^2=4$ with $-2^2=-4$.

## Worked examples
$2^3\times2^4=2^7=128$.

$5^7/5^3=5^4=625$.

$3^{-2}=1/3^2=1/9$.
