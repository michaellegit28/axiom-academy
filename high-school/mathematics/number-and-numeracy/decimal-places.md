## Learning objectives
Identify decimal places, round correctly, preserve trailing zeros when they communicate precision, and distinguish decimal places from significant figures.

## Decimal places
A decimal place is a position after the decimal point. In $7.3864$, the first decimal place is tenths (3), the second is hundredths (8), and so on.

## Rounding method
1. Identify the last decimal digit to keep.
2. Look at the next digit.
3. Round up if it is 5 or more; otherwise keep the last digit unchanged.

Thus $7.3864$ to 2 decimal places is $7.39$ because the third decimal digit is 6.

## Preserving precision
$4.5$ and $4.50$ have the same numerical value but can communicate different stated precision. In measurement, retain requested zeros.

## Negative values
Round the magnitude using the same rule, then retain the sign. For example, $-3.746$ to 2 decimal places is $-3.75$.

## Decimal places and significant figures
Decimal places count positions after the point; significant figures count meaningful digits from the first non-zero digit. For example, $0.00456$ has 5 decimal places but 3 significant figures.

## Common mistakes
- Looking at the wrong digit when rounding.
- Dropping a zero when a fixed number of decimal places is requested.
- Confusing decimal places with significant figures.
