## Learning objectives
Evaluate roots, simplify radicals using perfect-square factors, perform basic radical operations, and connect roots with indices.

## Roots
A square root of $a$ is a number whose square is $a$. Thus $\sqrt{49}=7$. A cube root reverses cubing: $\sqrt[3]{27}=3$.

For positive real numbers, the symbol $\sqrt a$ denotes the principal, non-negative square root. The equation $x^2=49$ has two solutions, $x=\pm7$.

## Simplifying square roots
Factor out perfect squares:
$$\sqrt{72}=\sqrt{36\times2}=6\sqrt2.$$
Similarly,
$$\sqrt{200}=\sqrt{100\times2}=10\sqrt2.$$

## Products and quotients
For non-negative $a,b$,
$$\sqrt a\sqrt b=\sqrt{ab}.$$
Also $\sqrt{a/b}=\sqrt a/\sqrt b$ when defined.

## Adding radicals
Only like radicals combine:
$$2\sqrt3+5\sqrt3=7\sqrt3.$$
But $\sqrt2+\sqrt3$ cannot be simplified to a single radical term.

## Fractional indices
$$a^{1/2}=\sqrt a,\qquad a^{1/3}=\sqrt[3]a.$$
This links roots to exponent laws.

## Common mistakes
- Writing $\sqrt{a+b}=\sqrt a+\sqrt b$; this is generally false.
- Forgetting the negative solution when solving $x^2=a$.
- Combining unlike radicals.

4. Simplify $\sqrt{72}$.

6. Simplify $\sqrt{12}+\sqrt{27}$.
7. Evaluate $64^{1/3}$.
8. Is $\sqrt2+\sqrt8=3\sqrt2$? Explain.
