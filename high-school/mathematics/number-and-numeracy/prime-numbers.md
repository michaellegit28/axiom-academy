## Learning objectives
Define prime and composite numbers, test whether a number is prime, and use prime factorisation.

## Prime and composite numbers
A prime number is an integer greater than 1 with exactly two positive factors: 1 and itself. Examples are $2,3,5,7,11$. A composite number has more than two positive factors.

The number 1 is neither prime nor composite. The only even prime is 2.

## Testing primality
To test $n$, it is sufficient to test prime divisors up to $\sqrt n$. If none divides $n$, then $n$ is prime.
For 29, $\sqrt{29}<6$, so test 2, 3 and 5. None divides 29; therefore 29 is prime.

## Prime factorisation
Every integer greater than 1 has a unique prime factorisation apart from order. For example,
$$90=2\times3^2\times5.$$

## Why primes matter
Prime factors provide the building blocks for whole numbers and are used to find HCF, LCM, simplify fractions and analyse divisibility.

## Common mistakes
- Treating 1 as prime.
- Testing divisors beyond what is necessary without understanding the square-root rule.
- Stopping a factorisation before all factors are prime.
