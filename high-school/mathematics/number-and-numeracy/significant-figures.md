## Learning objectives
Identify significant figures, distinguish significant from placeholder zeros, and round values correctly to a specified number of significant figures.

## What is significant?
Significant figures record meaningful digits in a measured or calculated value. Start counting at the first non-zero digit. Zeros between non-zero digits are significant; leading zeros are not.

Thus $0.00482$ has 3 significant figures, while $0.02050$ has 4.

Trailing zeros can be ambiguous in whole numbers. Scientific notation removes that ambiguity: $4.82\times10^3$ clearly has 3 significant figures.

## Rounding
To round to 3 significant figures, keep the first three significant digits and inspect the next digit. If it is 5 or more, increase the last kept digit.
$$38,742\to38,700$$

## Significant figures versus decimal places
Significant figures count meaningful digits overall. Decimal places count positions after the decimal point. They are not interchangeable.

For example, $12.345$ to 2 decimal places is $12.35$, but to 2 significant figures it is $12$.

## Measurement and precision
The appropriate number of significant figures should reflect the precision of the measurement or the context. More digits do not automatically mean more accuracy.

## Common mistakes
- Counting leading zeros as significant.
- Confusing decimal places with significant figures.
- Rounding too early in a multi-step calculation.
