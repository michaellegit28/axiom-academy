## Learning objectives
You should be able to use decimal place value, compare and order decimals, calculate with them, convert between decimals and fractions, and interpret terminating decimals.

## Place value
After the decimal point the places are tenths, hundredths, thousandths and so on. In $4.582$, the digits represent $4+5/10+8/100+2/1000$.
Trailing zeros do not change a value: $0.7=0.70=0.700$.

## Comparing and ordering
Align decimal points and compare digits from left to right. Thus $0.605>0.58$ because $0.580<0.605$.

## Adding and subtracting
Align decimal points:
$$4.75+2.608=7.358.$$

## Multiplication
Multiply as whole numbers, then place the decimal point using the total number of decimal places. For example,
$$6.4\times0.5=3.2.$$

## Division
Make the divisor a whole number by shifting the decimal point equally in both numbers. For example,
$$7.2\div0.6=72\div6=12.$$

## Converting fractions and decimals
A denominator of $10,100,1000,\ldots$ gives a direct conversion. For other fractions, divide numerator by denominator. For example, $3/8=0.375$.

## Terminating decimals
A decimal that ends is terminating and is rational. Every terminating decimal can be written over a power of 10, then simplified.

## Money and measurement
Decimals are used for exact monetary amounts, lengths, masses and other measurements. Always keep units and an appropriate precision.

## Common mistakes
- Comparing digits without aligning place value.
- Forgetting that leading zeros do not affect value.
- Placing a multiplication decimal point incorrectly.
- Treating a rounded decimal as exact when the context says it is an approximation.

## Worked examples
**1.** $0.42=42/100=21/50$.

**2.** $12.5\times0.08=1.000=1$.

**3.** $4.2\div0.07=420\div7=60$.

## Key review
Decimal place value controls size. Align decimal points for addition/subtraction, account for decimal places in multiplication, and use equivalent fractions or division for conversion.
