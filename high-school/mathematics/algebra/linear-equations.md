<section class="section-block page-content-start study-lesson"><div class="section-heading"><div><span class="eyebrow">High School · Mathematics · Algebra</span><h1>Linear Equations</h1><p>Use inverse operations to solve equations and check solutions.</p></div></div><article class="content-card"><h2>What is a linear equation?</h2><p>A linear equation has a variable whose highest power is 1. A simple example is 3x + 2 = 14.</p><h2>Solving equations</h2><p>Use inverse operations while maintaining equality on both sides. For 3x + 2 = 14, subtract 2 to get 3x = 12, then divide by 3: <strong>x = 4</strong>.</p><h2>Equations with brackets</h2><p>Expand brackets first, then collect like terms and isolate the variable.</p><h2>Checking</h2><p>Substitute the solution back into the original equation. For x = 4, 3(4) + 2 = 14, so the solution is correct.</p><h2>Check your understanding</h2><ol><li>Solve 5x - 7 = 18.</li><li>Solve 2(x + 3) = 16.</li><li>Explain why the same operation must be applied to both sides of an equation.</li></ol></article></section>
