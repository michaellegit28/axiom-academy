      <h1>Algebra Study</h1>
      <p>Build the foundations of algebra step by step, with clear explanations and worked examples.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="study-grid">
    <article class="study-card">
      <h2>1. Introduction to Algebra</h2>
      <p>Algebra uses symbols and letters to represent numbers and relationships. An algebraic statement can describe a quantity even when its value is unknown.</p>
      <h3>Key ideas</h3>
      <ul><li>A variable represents a value that may be unknown or change.</li><li>A constant is a fixed value.</li><li>A coefficient is a number multiplying a variable.</li><li>A term is a number, variable, or product joined by multiplication.</li></ul>
      <div class="example"><strong>Example:</strong> In <code>5x + 3</code>, <code>x</code> is the variable, <code>5</code> is the coefficient, and <code>3</code> is the constant.</div>
    </article>

    <article class="study-card">
      <h2>2. Variables &amp; Constants</h2>
      <p>Variables let us describe unknown quantities. A variable can take different values, while a constant keeps the same value within an expression.</p>
      <h3>Worked example</h3>
      <p>If <code>x = 4</code>, then <code>3x + 2 = 3(4) + 2 = 14</code>.</p>
      <p><strong>Remember:</strong> multiplication is usually written without the multiplication sign, so <code>3 × x</code> becomes <code>3x</code>.</p>
    </article>

    <article class="study-card">
      <h2>3. Algebraic Expressions</h2>
      <p>An expression combines numbers, variables and operations but does not contain an equals sign.</p>
      <h3>Like terms</h3>
      <p>Like terms have the same variables raised to the same powers. Their coefficients can be combined.</p>
      <div class="example"><strong>Example:</strong> <code>4x + 3x - 2 = 7x - 2</code>.</div>
    </article>

    <article class="study-card">
      <h2>4. Simplifying Expressions</h2>
      <p>To simplify, combine like terms and apply the distributive property where necessary.</p>
      <div class="example"><strong>Example:</strong> <code>2(x + 3) + x = 2x + 6 + x = 3x + 6</code>.</div>
      <p>Always distribute multiplication across every term inside parentheses before combining like terms.</p>
    </article>

    <article class="study-card">
      <h2>5. Linear Equations</h2>
      <p>An equation states that two expressions have equal values. Solving an equation means finding the value that makes the statement true.</p>
      <h3>Worked example</h3>
      <p><code>2x + 5 = 13</code></p>
      <ol><li>Subtract 5 from both sides: <code>2x = 8</code>.</li><li>Divide both sides by 2: <code>x = 4</code>.</li></ol>
      <p>The same operation must be applied to both sides to preserve equality.</p>
    </article>

    <article class="study-card">
      <h2>6. Inequalities</h2>
      <p>Inequalities compare quantities using symbols such as <code>&lt;</code>, <code>&gt;</code>, <code>≤</code> and <code>≥</code>.</p>
      <div class="example"><strong>Example:</strong> <code>2x + 1 &gt; 7</code> gives <code>2x &gt; 6</code>, so <code>x &gt; 3</code>.</div>
      <p><strong>Important:</strong> when multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative number, reverse the inequality sign.</p>
    </article>
  </div>

  <article class="study-card study-summary">
    <h2>Algebra Quick Review</h2>
    <ul><li>Identify variables, coefficients, constants and terms.</li><li>Combine only like terms.</li><li>Use the distributive property carefully.</li><li>Keep equations balanced by doing the same operation on both sides.</li><li>Reverse an inequality sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative number.</li></ul>
  </article>
