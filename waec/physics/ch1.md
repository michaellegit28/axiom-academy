## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:
- Identify fundamental and derived quantities in the SI system
- Convert between units using dimensional consistency
- Use measuring instruments (vernier caliper, micrometer screw gauge) correctly
- Record measurements to the correct number of significant figures
- Calculate absolute and percentage errors from repeated measurements
- Answer WAEC-style theory and objective questions on measurement

---

## 1. Fundamental and Derived Quantities

**Fundamental quantities** are measured directly. There are seven in the SI system:

| Quantity | Unit | Symbol |
|----------|------|--------|
| Length | metre | m |
| Mass | kilogram | kg |
| Time | second | s |
| Electric current | ampere | A |
| Temperature | kelvin | K |
| Amount of substance | mole | mol |
| Luminous intensity | candela | cd |

**Derived quantities** are defined in terms of fundamentals:

- **Velocity** = displacement / time → m/s
- **Acceleration** = velocity / time → m/s²
- **Force** = mass × acceleration → kg·m/s² = **newton (N)**
- **Energy** = force × distance → kg·m²/s² = **joule (J)**
- **Power** = energy / time → kg·m²/s³ = **watt (W)**

> **WAEC Tip:** Always write units in calculations. A number without a unit is meaningless in physics.

## 2. Measurement Instruments

### Vernier Caliper

A vernier caliper measures to **0.01 cm** (0.1 mm) precision.

**Reading = Main scale reading + (Vernier scale coincidence × least count)**

**Example:** Main scale reads 2.3 cm. The 4th vernier division coincides with a main scale division.

$$
\text{Reading} = 2.3\,\text{cm} + (4 \times 0.01\,\text{cm}) = 2.34\,\text{cm}
$$

### Micrometer Screw Gauge

A micrometer measures to **0.01 mm** (0.001 cm) precision.

**Reading = Sleeve reading + (Thimble reading × 0.01 mm)**

**Example:** Sleeve shows 5.5 mm. Thimble shows 28 divisions.

$$
\text{Reading} = 5.5\,\text{mm} + (28 \times 0.01\,\text{mm}) = 5.78\,\text{mm}
$$

> **Common error:** Forgetting to check for zero error. If the micrometer reads 0.03 mm when closed, subtract this from all measurements.

## 3. Significant Figures and Decimal Places

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| All non-zero digits are significant | 3.45 has 3 sig figs |
| Zeros between non-zero digits are significant | 3.05 has 3 sig figs |
| Leading zeros are not significant | 0.0045 has 2 sig figs |
| Trailing zeros after a decimal are significant | 3.500 has 4 sig figs |

**For multiplication/division:** The result has the same number of sig figs as the least precise measurement.

**Example:** $2.5 \times 3.42 = 8.6$ (not 8.55), because 2.5 has only 2 sig figs.

## 4. Errors in Measurement

### Types of Error

| Type | Cause | How to reduce |
|------|-------|---------------|
| **Random** | Unpredictable fluctuations | Take repeated readings, average |
| **Systematic** | Faulty instrument or method | Calibrate instrument, check zero error |
| **Parallax** | Eye not perpendicular to scale | Read at right angles |

### Calculating Error

**Absolute error** = |measured value − true value| (or half the range for repeated readings)

**Percentage error** = $\displaystyle \frac{\text{absolute error}}{\text{measured value}} \times 100\%$

**Example:** Five readings of a length: 2.34, 2.36, 2.35, 2.34, 2.36 cm.

- Mean = $\displaystyle \frac{2.34 + 2.36 + 2.35 + 2.34 + 2.36}{5} = 2.35$ cm
- Range = 2.36 − 2.34 = 0.02 cm
- Absolute error = $\displaystyle \frac{0.02}{2} = 0.01$ cm
- Result = $2.35 \pm 0.01$ cm
- Percentage error = $\displaystyle \frac{0.01}{2.35} \times 100\% = 0.43\%$

## 5. Unit Conversions

Use **dimensional analysis** — multiply by conversion factors written as fractions equal to 1.

**Example:** Convert 72 km/h to m/s.

$$
72\,\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}} \times \frac{1000\,\text{m}}{1\,\text{km}} \times \frac{1\,\text{h}}{3600\,\text{s}}
= \frac{72 \times 1000}{3600}\,\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} = 20\,\text{m/s}
$$

> **WAEC Shortcut:** km/h → m/s: divide by 3.6. m/s → km/h: multiply by 3.6.

## 📝 WAEC Practice Questions

### Section A — Short Answer (2–3 marks each)

1. State two fundamental quantities and their SI units.
2. Distinguish between accuracy and precision.
3. A micrometer screw gauge has a zero error of +0.02 mm. What is the correct diameter of a wire if the gauge reads 3.45 mm?
4. Convert 5.0 g/cm³ to kg/m³.
5. The period of a pendulum is measured as 2.0 s, 2.2 s, 1.9 s, 2.1 s, and 2.0 s. Calculate the mean value and the absolute error.

### Section B — Essay (5–6 marks each)

6. (a) Explain what is meant by a derived quantity. Give two examples.
   (b) A student measures the density of a metal block. State four possible sources of error and how each can be minimized.

7. (a) Describe how you would use a vernier caliper to measure the internal diameter of a test tube.
   (b) The main scale reading is 1.2 cm and the 7th vernier division coincides. Calculate the diameter.

### Objective — Multiple Choice

8. Which of the following is a fundamental quantity?
   - A. Velocity
   - B. Force
   - C. Mass
   - D. Energy

9. The least count of a micrometer screw gauge is:
   - A. 0.1 mm
   - B. 0.01 mm
   - C. 0.001 mm
   - D. 1.0 mm

10. A measurement recorded as $5.60$ cm has how many significant figures?
    - A. 2
    - B. 3
    - C. 4
    - D. 5

---

## ✅ Answers

8. **C** (Mass)  
9. **B** (0.01 mm)  
10. **B** (3 — the trailing zero after the decimal is significant)
